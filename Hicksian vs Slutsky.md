## Q. Explain the Hicksian and Slutsky methods of Price Effect. Prove that Price Effect = Substitution Effect + Income Effect. **(10 marks)**

### 1. Meaning of Price Effect

When the **price of one good changes** (money income of the consumer and the price of the other good remaining constant), the consumer's equilibrium moves from one point to another. The resulting **change in the quantity purchased of that good** is called the **Price Effect (Total Price Effect)**.

This total effect is made of **two parts**:

* **Substitution Effect (SE):** When a good becomes relatively cheaper, the consumer **substitutes** it for the now relatively dearer good, keeping his **satisfaction (real income) the same**.
* **Income Effect (IE):** A fall in price raises the consumer's **real income** (purchasing power); with the extra purchasing power he buys more (for a normal good).

So, conceptually: **Price Effect = Substitution Effect + Income Effect.** The two famous methods of separating these two parts are the **Hicksian Method** and the **Slutsky Method**.

---

### 2. Hicksian Method (J.R. Hicks — satisfaction kept constant)

**Assumptions:** Two goods X and Y; money income fixed; price of X **falls**; ordinal utility.

**Step-by-step logic (follow Figure 1):**

1. **Original equilibrium:** Budget line **AB** is tangent to indifference curve **IC₁** at **R**. The consumer buys **OX₁** of good X.
2. **Price of X falls:** The budget line pivots (from point A) to the flatter line **AB₁**. The consumer *would* now move to a higher curve.
3. **Taking away income (compensating variation):** To find the *pure* substitution effect, Hicks takes away just enough money income so that the consumer comes **back to the original curve IC₁**. This gives an **imaginary line HG, parallel to AB₁, tangent to IC₁ at R′**. The consumer now buys **OX₂**.
4. **Movement R → R′ (on the same IC₁) = Substitution Effect = X₁X₂.** It is always positive — the cheaper good X replaces Y.
5. **Giving the income back:** The budget line now shifts out from HG to AB₁ (parallel shift) and the consumer reaches the higher curve **IC₂** at **R₂**, buying **OX₃**.
6. **Movement R′ → R₂ = Income Effect = X₂X₃.**
7. **Total movement R → R₂ = Price Effect = X₁X₃.**

---

### 3. Slutsky Method (E. Slutsky — purchasing power kept constant)

Slutsky does **not** keep satisfaction constant. He takes away just enough money income so that the consumer **can still buy the ORIGINAL bundle R if he wishes**. The compensation is measured by the **cost difference** of the old bundle at the two price sets.

**Step-by-step logic (follow Figure 2):**

1. **Original equilibrium:** Same as before — **AB** tangent to **IC₁** at **R**; consumer buys **OX₁**.
2. **Price of X falls:** Budget line becomes **AB₁**.
3. **Compensated line:** Draw **LN through R, parallel to AB₁** (income is reduced so that the old combination R is just affordable). On this line X is relatively cheaper, so the consumer re-arranges purchases and moves to **R′**, buying **OX₂**.
4. **Movement R → R′ = Substitution Effect = X₁X₂.** Note: **R′ lies on a higher curve IC₂** (orange) — because Slutsky keeps *purchasing power* constant, *not satisfaction* constant.
5. **Income restored:** The line shifts from LN out to AB₁; the consumer reaches the higher curve **IC₃** at **R₃**, buying **OX₃**.
6. **Movement R′ → R₃ = Income Effect = X₂X₃; total R → R₃ = Price Effect = X₁X₃.**

---

### 4. Difference between the two methods

| Basis | Hicksian Method | Slutsky Method |
|---|---|---|
| What is kept constant | **Satisfaction** (consumer stays on old IC₁) | **Purchasing power** (he can still buy old bundle R) |
| Compensated line | **HG** — tangent to old IC₁ | **LN** — passes **through** old equilibrium R |
| Position of R′ | On the **same** IC₁ | On a **higher** IC₂ |
| Size of Substitution Effect | Smaller | Larger |
| Based on | Compensating variation in income | Cost difference of the old bundle |

---

### 5. Proof that Price Effect = Substitution Effect + Income Effect

**(a) Diagrammatic proof (from both figures):**
The three points R, R′ and R₂/R₃ lie one after another towards the right on the X-axis. Therefore the three distances simply add up:

**OX₃ − OX₁ = (OX₂ − OX₁) + (OX₃ − OX₂)**
⇒ **X₁X₃ = X₁X₂ + X₂X₃**
⇒ **Price Effect = Substitution Effect + Income Effect.** (Proved)

This holds in *both* figures — only the split between SE and IE differs (Slutsky's SE is larger).

**(b) Algebraic proof (Slutsky equation):**
Let X = f(Pₓ, M). If Pₓ changes, compensate income by ΔM = X·ΔPₓ (so the old bundle stays just affordable). Then the total change in X splits into two parts:

ΔX = (change due to Pₓ at compensated income) + (change due to the income adjustment at new prices)

Dividing by ΔPₓ and taking the limit gives the **Slutsky equation**:

**(∂X/∂Pₓ) = (∂X/∂Pₓ)|substitution − X·(∂X/∂M)**

* First term = **Substitution Effect** (always negative for own price).
* Second term = **Income Effect** (a rise in Pₓ cuts real income by X; multiplied by the income-response of X).

Hence: **Total Price Effect = Substitution Effect + Income Effect.** (Proved)

**(c) Interpretation:** For a **fall** in Pₓ, SE is always positive; IE is positive for a **normal good** → PE positive. For an **inferior** good IE is negative but weak → PE still positive. Only for a **Giffen good** does the negative IE outweigh SE → PE negative.

---

### 6. Conclusion

The price effect of a price change is never a single force: it is the **sum of the substitution effect** (relative-cheapness effect, isolated by Hicks through a compensated line **tangent to the old IC₁**, and by Slutsky through a compensated line **through the old equilibrium R**) **and the income effect** (real-income effect, shown by the final shift to the higher indifference curve). Diagrammatically, **X₁X₃ = X₁X₂ + X₂X₃**, and algebraically the Slutsky equation confirms: **Price Effect = Substitution Effect + Income Effect.**
