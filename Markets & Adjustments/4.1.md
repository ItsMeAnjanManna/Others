# Chapter 4: Market and Adjustments

## Section 4.1 – Introduction

What is this section about?

This section introduces how the market economy developed and explains the role of households, firms and government in an economy.

### 1. Development of the market economy

Human society gradually moved through different stages of economic development:

1. Agriculture: People started cultivating land and producing food.
2. Improved productivity: Better tools and technology helped people produce more goods.
3. Division of labour: People started specialising in different occupations, such as farming, weaving and carpentry.
4. Exchange of goods: People started exchanging their surplus products with others.
5. Market economy: Gradually, organised markets developed where buyers and sellers exchanged goods and services using money.

### 2. What is a market economy?

A market economy is an economic system where the forces of demand and supply determine the prices of goods and services.

Example:

- When demand for mangoes increases but supply remains limited, their prices generally increase.
- When supply increases but demand remains low, their prices generally decrease.

Thus, demand and supply play an important role in determining market prices.

### 3. What will we study in this chapter?

The chapter mainly discusses three areas:

- Decision-makers: Households, firms and government and their roles in an economy.
- Individual markets: Different markets, such as goods markets and factor markets.
- Interlinking of markets: How changes in one market affect other markets.

### Quick revision

Remember these three important points:

1. Market economies developed through improvements in production, technology and exchange.
2. Demand and supply determine market prices.
3. Households, firms and government are the major economic decision-makers.
