# Section 4.4.1 – Interlinking of Individual Markets

This section explains how different markets are connected and how changes in one market can affect other markets.

Although individual markets may be separate, they are often interlinked.

## 1. All products compete for consumers' income

Consumers have limited income, so they must decide how to spend their money.

When people spend more on one product, they may have less money available for other products.

Example: If someone spends more money on a new mobile phone, they may reduce their spending on clothes or entertainment.

However, demand for complementary goods can also increase together.

Example: Increased demand for electric vehicles may increase demand for charging stations and batteries.

## 2. Existence of arbitrage

Arbitrage means purchasing a product in a market where its price is low and selling it in another market where its price is higher to earn a profit.

Example:

- A trader purchases a product for ₹500 in Market A.
- The same product sells for ₹700 in Market B.
- The trader can earn ₹200 before other costs by selling it in Market B.

This buying and selling activity connects the two markets.

## 3. Interlinking of markets

Different markets become interconnected when the same individual or organisation participates in multiple markets as a buyer or seller.

Example: Rural markets

Consider a large landowner in a village.

The landowner may participate in several markets:

- Credit market: Provides loans to small farmers.
- Product market: Purchases agricultural products from farmers.
- Labour market: Hires agricultural workers.
- Land market: Rents or leases agricultural land.

Therefore, these markets are interconnected because the same individual participates in all of them.

The textbook also mentions that large multinational companies can operate in several markets and influence different industries.

### Quick Revision

| Concept                 | Meaning                                                                 |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Competition for income  | Different products compete for consumers' limited income                |
| Arbitrage               | Buying at a lower price and selling at a higher price in another market |
| Interlinking of markets | Different markets connected through common buyers or sellers            |

Key takeaway: Markets do not always operate independently. Consumer spending, arbitrage and participation of common buyers and sellers connect different markets.
