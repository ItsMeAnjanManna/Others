# Section 4.4 – Individual Markets and Separation of Individual Markets

This section explains that different goods and services have their own markets. It also explains why markets are sometimes separated from one another.

## 1. What is an individual market?

An individual market is a market where buyers and sellers exchange a particular good or service.

Examples:

- Vegetable market
- Stock market
- Crude oil market
- Foreign exchange market
- Consumer goods market

For a market to exist, there must be both demand and supply for a product.

## 2. Types of individual markets

The textbook divides individual markets into two categories:

A. Goods Market

Where consumers buy and sell finished goods and services.

Examples: Rice, clothes, vegetables and toothpaste.

B. Factor Market

Where factors of production are bought and sold.

Examples:

- Labour
- Land
- Raw materials
- Machines and capital

Remember: Goods markets deal with final goods, whereas factor markets deal with resources used in production.

## 3. Separation of individual markets

Markets can be separated from one another due to three major factors:

A. Type of products sold

Different products have different markets.

Example: The computer hardware market is different from the computer software market.

B. Natural barriers

Geographical distance and agro-climatic conditions can separate markets.

Example: The Japanese electronic goods market is geographically separated from the Indian market, making direct buying and selling more difficult.

C. Government-created barriers

Governments can restrict trade between countries through:

- Tariff barriers: Taxes imposed on imported or exported goods.
- Non-tariff barriers: Restrictions such as import quotas that limit the quantity of goods entering a country.

These restrictions can make it difficult for goods to move freely between markets.

### Quick Revision

| Concept             | Meaning                                 |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Individual market   | Market for a particular good or service |
| Goods market        | Exchange of consumer goods and services |
| Factor market       | Exchange of factors of production       |
| Natural barriers    | Geographical or climatic restrictions   |
| Tariff barriers     | Taxes on international trade            |
| Non-tariff barriers | Trade restrictions other than taxes     |

Key takeaway: Individual markets are formed for different goods and services. They may be separated by product differences, natural barriers or government restrictions.
