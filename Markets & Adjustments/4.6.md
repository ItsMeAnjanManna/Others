## 4.6 Market and Non-market Sectors

An economy can be divided into two broad sectors based on whether goods and services are provided through the market or outside it.

### 1. Market Sector

The market sector includes all economic activities where goods and services are produced and sold at a price.

- Producers sell goods and services to earn revenue and profit.
- Consumers purchase them by paying money.
- Prices are generally determined by demand and supply.

Examples:

- A shop selling groceries.
- A private hospital charging patients.
- A company selling mobile phones.
- A restaurant selling food.

### 2. Non-market Sector

The non-market sector includes activities where goods and services are provided without being sold at a market price, or where payment is not the main objective.

- Activities may be carried out by the government, charitable organisations or households.
- The main objective is often public welfare or meeting social needs rather than earning profit.
- Services may be free or provided at a subsidised price.

Examples:

- Government schools providing free education.
- Public parks maintained for everyone.
- Unpaid household work, such as cooking and caring for family members.
- Charitable organisations providing free food.

### Difference between Market and Non-market Sectors

| Market Sector                                         | Non-market Sector                                            |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Goods and services are sold at a price.               | Goods and services are generally not sold at market prices.  |
| Earning revenue and profit is an important objective. | Social welfare or meeting needs is often the main objective. |
| Prices are generally influenced by demand and supply. | Services may be free or subsidised.                          |
| Example: Private hospital.                            | Example: Government hospital providing free services.        |

### Important Point

Non-market activities are also economically valuable, even if they do not involve monetary transactions.

For example, when a parent cooks food for their family, it is useful economic activity, although no money changes hands.

In short: The market sector operates mainly through buying and selling, whereas the non-market sector provides goods and services without relying primarily on market transactions.
