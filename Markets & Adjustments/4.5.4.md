# Section 4.5.4 – Free and Controlled Markets

This section explains how markets are classified based on the level of government intervention in economic activities.

There are two types of markets:

1. Free Market
2. Controlled Market

## 1. Free Market Economy

A free market economy is an economic system where consumers and producers make their own economic decisions without direct government interference.

Main characteristics:

1. Producers independently decide what and how much to produce.
2. Consumers freely decide what to purchase.
3. Private ownership of property is allowed.
4. Prices are determined by the interaction of demand and supply.
5. Resources are allocated through the price mechanism.

A free market economy is also known as a Laissez-faire Economy.

Example: A clothing manufacturer decides what clothes to produce based on consumer demand and expected profit.

### Limitations of a Free Market

The textbook identifies some problems:

- Income inequality: Wealth may become concentrated among a few people.
- Inflation: Rising prices of essential goods can affect poor people more severely.
- Business fluctuations: Economic activity may experience ups and downs, leading to recession.
- Misallocation of resources: Producers may focus on more profitable products rather than socially necessary products.

These problems indicate the need for government intervention in certain situations.

## 2. Controlled Market Economy

A controlled market economy is an economic system where the government determines important economic decisions such as:

- What and how much to produce.
- How resources should be distributed.
- What prices should be charged.

In this system, prices are not determined entirely by market demand and supply.

Instead, the government or a central planning authority decides the allocation of resources and prices.

Example: The former Soviet Union (USSR), before 1991, followed a centrally planned economic system.

The Central Planning Authority (GOSPLAN) decided production quantities, resource allocation and administered prices.

## 3. Differences Between Free and Controlled Markets

| Basis                 | Free Market                    | Controlled Market                      |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| Price mechanism       | Free price mechanism           | Administered price mechanism           |
| Decision-maker        | Consumers and producers        | Central Planning Authority             |
| Economic system       | Capitalist economy             | Socialist economy                      |
| Resource allocation   | Through demand and supply      | Through government planning            |
| Production decisions  | Producers decide independently | Government determines production plans |
| Consumption decisions | Consumers decide freely        | Consumer choices may be restricted     |

## Quick Revision

| Concept              | Meaning                                                 |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Free market          | Market with independent consumer and producer decisions |
| Laissez-faire        | Minimum government interference                         |
| Controlled market    | Government controls major economic decisions            |
| Free price mechanism | Prices determined by demand and supply                  |
| Administered price   | Price determined by government authority                |
| GOSPLAN              | Central Planning Authority of the former USSR           |

Key takeaway: In a free market, demand and supply determine prices and guide economic decisions. In a controlled market, the government or central planning authority makes major production, distribution and pricing decisions.
