## Section 4.5.2: Goods and Factor Markets (Teaching Guide)

**The Big Picture:** Markets can be divided into two main types based on *what* is being bought and sold:

1. **Goods Market:** Where finished products (like food, clothes, or cars) are bought by consumers and sold by businesses.
2. **Factor Market:** Where the *inputs* needed to make those products (like workers, land, and machines) are bought/hired by businesses.

The goods market is the system where buyers and sellers trade different types of goods according to price, driven by both demand and supply. To teach this easily, split consumer goods into two everyday categories:

* **Non-Durable Consumer Goods (Fig. 4(a)):** Goods that **cannot** be stored for long and are used up right away (e.g., milk, bread, vegetables, fruits, eggs).


* **Durable Consumer Goods (Fig. 4(b)):** Goods that **can** be stored and used repeatedly over a long period (e.g., cars, refrigerators, washing machines, air conditioners). Their demand comes from two sources: **replacement demand** (replacing old/worn-out items) and **fresh demand** (buying for the first time).

### Part 2: The Factor Market — Fig. 5(a) & Fig. 5(b)

<img width="1024" height="572" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/de1cadc6-b5fe-4b84-9ab2-a7b7647ce4ca" />


To make any good, firms need **Factors of Production**, which fall into four groups:

1. **Land:** Not just soil, but all gifts of nature whose supply humans can control/regulate (e.g., coal, minerals). *Teaching tip:* Point out that **sunlight and rainfall are NOT counted as land** because humans cannot control their supply.
2. **Labour:** Human physical or mental effort used in production. *Teaching tip:* Animal effort does not count as labour.
3. **Capital:** "Produced means of production"—man-made inputs (like machines or tools) used to make other goods, not consumed directly.
4. **Organisation (Entrepreneurship):** The entrepreneur who takes initiative—hiring workers, renting land, arranging capital, and running the business.


#### Key Concept: Derived Demand

Unlike consumer goods, firms do not hire workers or buy machines for direct enjoyment—they only demand them when consumers want the final products those factors make. That is why factor demand is called **derived (or indirect) demand**, linking the goods market and the factor market.
