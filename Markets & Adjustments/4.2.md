# Section 4.2 – Evolution of Market Economy: The Price System

What is this section about?

This section explains how the market economy developed through three stages of human economic development.

## 1. Pre-agricultural stage (Hunting and food gathering)

People depended on hunting animals and collecting naturally available food.

Main characteristics:

- People collected food mainly for survival.
- Natural resources were widely available.
- Private property was not developed.
- People did not focus on accumulating wealth.
- Material goods and technology were limited.

Example: People hunted animals and collected fruits to fulfil their daily needs.

## 2. Stage of settled cultivation

People gradually stopped their nomadic lifestyle and started agriculture and permanent settlements.

Main characteristics:

- Agriculture and domestication of animals developed.
- Private property emerged.
- Scarcity of resources created the problem of choice.
- Agricultural and labour productivity improved.
- Surplus food production started.
- People began exchanging surplus goods through the barter system.
- New occupations emerged, resulting in division and specialisation of labour.
- Total production increased due to improved efficiency.

## 3. Industrial Revolution and expansion of market

The Industrial Revolution transformed an agricultural economy into an industrial economy.

Main developments:

- Introduction of machines such as steam engines and spinning jennies.
- Development of factory-based production.
- Large-scale production reduced average production costs.
- Industrial employment and people's income increased.
- Agricultural productivity improved.
- Demand for different consumer goods increased, expanding markets.

### Four phases of Industrial Revolution

| Phase               | Main development                  |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| First (1760–1830)   | Coal and steam power              |
| Second (1870–1968)  | Electricity, gas and fossil fuels |
| Third (1969–1999)   | Nuclear energy and computers      |
| Fourth (Since 2000) | Internet, AI and IoT              |

### Quick revision

The evolution of the market economy can be remembered as:

1. Hunting and food gathering.
2. Settled agriculture and surplus production.
3. Barter system and specialisation.
4. Industrialisation and large-scale production.
5. Expansion of modern markets.

Key takeaway: Improvements in productivity, specialisation, technology and exchange gradually transformed primitive society into a modern market economy.
