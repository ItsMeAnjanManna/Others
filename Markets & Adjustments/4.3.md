# Section 4.3 – The Decision-makers: Households, Firms and Central Authorities

This section explains the three major decision-makers in a market economy:

1. Households
2. Firms
3. Government (Central Authorities)

Each plays a different role in the economy.

## 4.3.1 Households as decision-makers

Households consist of earning and non-earning members.

They perform two important economic functions:

A. Consumers

- Households create demand for goods and services.
- They purchase products such as food, clothes, toothpaste and other daily necessities.
- Their objective is to maximise satisfaction (utility) within their available income.

B. Suppliers of factors of production

- Households supply labour, land and capital to firms.
- In return, they receive wages, rent and interest.

Example: A person works in a company and receives a monthly salary. The person is supplying labour to the company and earning income.

Important concept: Households try to maximise their utility subject to their budget constraint.

## 4.3.2 Firms as decision-makers

Firms are production units that produce and supply goods and services.

Their two major functions are:

- Supply goods and services to the market.
- Create demand for factors of production such as labour, land and capital.

Main objectives of firms:

- Maximise production with available resources.
- Minimise production costs.
- Maximise profit.
- Increase sales revenue and market share.

Example: A clothing company hires workers, purchases raw materials and manufactures clothes to sell in the market.

Firms decide how much to produce and how many resources to employ.

## 4.3.3. Government as decision-maker

The government plays an important role in regulating and supporting the market economy.

Major functions:

1\. Regulation: Creates laws and rules to protect private property and maintain fair market practices.

2\. Economic policies: Uses taxation, budgetary policy, monetary policy and trade policies to influence economic activities.

3\. Demand creation: Purchases goods such as stationery, vehicles and equipment for government activities.

4\. Investment: Invests in infrastructure such as roads, railways and bridges to encourage economic development.

5\. Public goods: Provides services such as defence and primary healthcare.

6\. Price regulation: Regulates the prices of essential commodities for public welfare.

Example: The government builds highways, provides public healthcare and regulates the prices of certain essential commodities.

### Important concept: Public goods

Public goods have two important characteristics:

- Non-rivalrous: One person's consumption does not reduce the availability for others.
- Non-excludable: People cannot easily be prevented from using them.

Example: National defence.

## Quick Revision

| Decision-maker | Main role                                      | Objective                               |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Households     | Consume goods and supply factors of production | Maximise utility                        |
| Firms          | Produce and supply goods and services          | Maximise profit / minimise cost         |
| Government     | Regulate the economy and provide public goods  | Public welfare and economic development |

Key takeaway: Households consume and supply resources, firms produce goods and services, and the government regulates and supports economic activities.
